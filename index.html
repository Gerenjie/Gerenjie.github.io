About

Measuring what a survey can and cannot see

I'm a PhD candidate in Astronomy at the University of Washington and the DiRAC Institute, advised by Mario Jurić. Since 2023 I have developed the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's solar system pipelines: I designed the pipeline that associates detections with known objects, and integrated the heliolinx linking algorithm into a new LSST discovery pipeline. The pipeline runs I'm responsible for have found more than 33,000 new asteroids.

My thesis, Debiasing the LSST Solar System Catalogs, aims to extend LSST's discoveries into a robust statistical constraint on the solar system by accounting not only for what we see, but what we should expect remains unseen.

Before Rubin I worked at the Minor Planet Center at the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, characterizing all-sky surveys and searching archival catalogs for distant objects. I studied math and computer science at Harvard.

Research

Finding the solar system, then correcting for what we missed

Pipeline lead2023 – present

Rubin's solar system pipelines

Rubin is designed to find millions of new solar system objects. I lead development of the pipelines that do it: associating each night's detections with known objects, and linking the leftover detections into new orbits using heliolinx. By the end of the ten-year survey, most detections of known asteroids and most discoveries of new ones will have come through these pipelines.

Rubin's early data are already turning up science, from a super-fast rotating asteroid in Rubin's first alerts to observations of interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS.

Thesis2022 – 2027

Debiasing the LSST solar system catalogs

A discovery list tells you what a survey caught, not what exists. I inject simulated asteroids into Rubin's image processing to measure, object by object, whether a source of a given brightness and motion would have been detected, and use that to recover the true populations behind the catalogs.

First author2025

Predicting what LSST will find

How many near-Earth objects, main-belt asteroids, Jupiter Trojans and trans-Neptunian objects will LSST discover, and how well will it characterize them? I led the simulation of the full survey's solar system yield, part of a series of predictions covering Centaurs, Neptune Trojans and Hildas.

First author2019 – 2025

A well-characterized search for Centaurs

At the Minor Planet Center I searched Pan-STARRS1 archival data for Centaurs with HelioLinC, and characterized the search completely enough to turn detections into population estimates. The work continued into a Pan-STARRS search for distant planets.
